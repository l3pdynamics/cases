The Cost of the Wrong Rate Card

Same operating model. Same occupancy ramp. Same expense methodology. Two different market-rate assumptions produced a five-year NOI swing wide enough to change what the sponsor underwrote to.

$28M–$33M
Difference in five-year net operating income between two rate-card assumptions, run through an identical four-version operating model — before a dollar of capital was committed.

The Situation

A development sponsor for a large-scale, ground-up multifamily project had two competing market-rate assumption sets in play for the same underlying five-year operating model. Both were plausible. Both had been built out in full detail — four parallel scenario versions apiece, tracking revenue, expenses, and NOI year by year.

The sponsor's question wasn't which model was "right." It was how much the choice actually mattered — whether picking one rate case over the other was a rounding difference or a decision that would change how the deal got underwritten and presented to capital.

What L3P Did

Reconciled two parallel four-version model sets — Base, Base Plus, and each with an embedded amenity services overlay — against source budget detail, then built a variance-decomposition layer that isolates two levers independently rather than blending them:

  • The rate-card lever — the pure effect of the assumption change itself, occupancy and expense methodology held constant
  • The amenity overlay lever — the net community impact of an embedded, cost-based service line, tested for consistency regardless of which rate case sits underneath it

Deltas were live-linked back to each model's source detail rather than calculated as static one-off figures, so the comparison holds up if either rate case is revised.

Method

1

Reconcile both model sets to source

Verified each of the eight version files (four scenarios × two rate cases) tied to its own budget detail worksheet before any comparison work began.

2

Build the cross-assumption variance layer

Constructed a live-linked comparison isolating the rate-card swing independent of scenario, so the same delta logic applies whether the sponsor is looking at the conservative or upside case.

3

Decompose the two levers separately

Held occupancy and expense methodology constant to isolate the pure rate-strategy effect, then held rate strategy constant to isolate the amenity overlay's net community impact — confirming each behaves independently of the other.

4

Deliver a defensible underwriting basis

Handed the sponsor a side-by-side view of what each rate case implies for five-year economics, with the assumption risk quantified rather than asserted.

The finding that mattered most wasn't which rate case was correct — it was that the amenity overlay's impact held steady regardless of which one the sponsor chose, while the rate-card choice itself was doing almost all the work. That gave the sponsor a clean way to present the range to capital: two defensible cases, one consistent story about what actually drives the number.
